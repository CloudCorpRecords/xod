## Proposition under synthesis

Should duration scaling (METR style horizons), pass^k reliability, and a judge validity protocol enter the commons, and under what guards?

## Current conclusion

Yes, as protocol amendments, adopted with the skeptical review's guards in full:

1. **Duration scaling** enters `docs/METRICS.md` as section 9. The 50 percent horizon is reported only for duration calibrated task sets, always named with task family and suite; durations state their source class (timed experts, calibrated estimates, author assertion); cross family horizon comparison is prohibited in claim language; failed fits report "horizon not estimable." No verdict requires a horizon.
2. **pass^k** enters METRICS section 2 beside confirmation and clean rates. Attempts are grouped only across ordinary reset boundaries with a carryover screen (memory, caches, destination state) reported in the packet; k is predeclared by consequence class (defaults 3, 5, 8 for C1, C2, C3, recorded as conventions); correlated groups are labeled or omitted. Trust language at C2 and C3 should cite pass^k where grouped attempts exist.
3. **Judge validity** enters `docs/PROTOCOLS.md` as Appendix J (MVVP): chance corrected agreement, position swap, repeated runs, anonymized and length controlled comparisons, and a degradation gold sample with predeclared minimum accuracy per dimension. A judge below gold threshold is excluded from gate evidence, with the exclusion recorded. No gate may rest on judge evidence alone where destination evidence is obtainable.

No claim is registered by this deliberation. The instruments are conventions of measurement, adopted the way METRICS sections 1 to 9 were adopted, and revisable by the same route. If field use later supports propositions about them (for example, that horizons predict Tier 6 field reliability), those propositions enter as claims through a new deliberation.

## Decisive arguments

Each instrument fills a named silence without touching a gate: the ladder could not express duration, could not express every attempt reliability, and had no rule for the judge derived numbers packets were certain to start quoting. The skeptic's contribution is what makes adoption safe: every guard converts a known abuse (suite free horizon quoting, correlated pass^k, unexcludable judges) into a recorded scoring failure rather than a matter of taste. Optional status is load bearing in the other direction: because no verdict depends on these instruments, their absence from a packet is a limitation to note, never a defect that invalidates prior work.

## Agreed facts

- No existing assessment used any of the three instruments; none requires reinterpretation.
- The k defaults and gold thresholds are conventions, revisable by note with reasons.
- External benchmarks remain task sources, not verdict authorities, inside this repository.

## Dissent preserved

The proposal's optimism that logistic fits will behave outside software tasks is preserved as an expectation, not a finding: the first non software horizon reported under section 10 should be treated as the fit's field test, and a "not estimable" result there is valuable evidence, not an embarrassment. If horizons prove estimable only in software like domains, section 10 narrows by amendment with this dissent as its starting point.

## Evidence that could change this synthesis

Packets exercising the instruments: a horizon that fails to fit outside software, pass^k groupings defeated by undetectable carryover, or a judge passing gold audits while disagreeing with destination evidence on gates. Any of these amends the instrument through a new note; none reopens the question of whether the silences needed instruments.
