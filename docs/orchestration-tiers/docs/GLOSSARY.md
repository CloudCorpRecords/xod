# Glossary

## Action

An operation intended to change state outside the model's immediate reply, such as writing a file, sending a message, changing a setting, creating a record, or starting a process.

## Agent system

The complete configuration being assessed: model, prompts, tools, memory, state, schedulers, interfaces, permissions, external services, and human oversight arrangements.

## Assessment

A dated application of the ladder and evidence standard to a specified system configuration and environment, producing gate by gate findings and verdicts.

## Authority

Permission to perform a class of action for a principal, within a scope, under stated approvals and limits. Authority is distinct from technical capability.

## Authority envelope

The explicit boundary of actions, destinations, amounts, durations, delegations, and conditions within which a system may act without new approval.

## Autonomy

The degree to which a system chooses and executes steps without contemporaneous human direction, described by scope, duration, reversibility, and escalation conditions.

## Borderline case

An observed behavior that resembles a tier but fails one or more mandatory criteria, making it useful for clarifying the boundary.

## Capability

What a system can do under at least some authentic conditions. Capability alone does not imply repeatability or trustworthiness.

## Claim

A bounded proposition about a system, configuration, environment, interval, and tier, with a verdict and evidence path.

## Claim registry

The maintained list of propositions under study, their statuses, and links to deliberation or evidence.

## Class A evidence

Independent direct evidence, such as an external destination record or independently captured observation.

## Class B evidence

Primary artifacts generated by the assessed system or its infrastructure, such as logs and state files.

## Class C evidence

Structured self report that identifies outcomes and evidence locations in a checkable form.

## Class D evidence

Narrative, promotional, or testimonial assertion without adequate underlying artifacts.

## Class E evidence

Missing, contradictory, invalid, or unusable evidence that limits or defeats a claim as stated.

## Confidence

A judgment about the strength and completeness of an assessment basis, reported separately from the verdict.

## Contention

Competition among agents, users, or processes for an exclusive or limited resource, such as one device, browser session, account, file, or communication channel.

## Deliberation chain

An ordered and linked set of append only notes through which a proposition is proposed, reviewed, challenged, supplemented, and synthesized.

## Delivery

The arrival of a result at its intended human or system destination in a usable form. Producing a result internally is not necessarily delivery.

## Destination read back

Observation of the intended state at the place where an action was supposed to have effect, performed after the action.

## Determinism of verdicts

The property that the same evidence packet under the same rubric yields the same verdict, with disagreements traceable to evidence, scope, or rubric interpretation.

## Durable state

Task or system state preserved outside a transient reasoning context so that work can survive interruption, restart, or delay.

## Evidence packet

The organized collection of claim, protocol, run index, artifacts, interventions, destination checks, failures, unknowns, and verdict derivation supporting an assessment.

## False positive

An apparent tier behavior that is credited too strongly because a mandatory property was absent, hidden human work supplied the result, or the artifact does not prove the asserted process.

## Fleet

A coordinated set of devices, agents, services, accounts, or principals treated as one operational environment.

## Gate

A mandatory criterion for a tier verdict. Gates are not offset by unrelated excellence.

## Grounding

The practice of tying statements and actions to an appropriate source or observed state rather than memory, plausibility, or model prior.

## Handoff

Transfer of work, context, authority, or responsibility from one agent, process, device, or human to another.

## Intervention

An action by a human or external actor during a run that materially assists, redirects, repairs, approves, or stops the work. Interventions must be recorded in serious assessments.

## Ladder order

The rule that lower tier regressions and defects are addressed before relying on higher tier claims, because higher orchestration depends on lower mandatory properties.

## Lease

A time bounded resource claim that expires unless renewed through a heartbeat or equivalent signal.

## Mandatory criterion

A property that must be observed for a tier to receive a PROVEN verdict under the model.

## Note

An append only contribution to a deliberation, with an identifier, author, role, proposition, links, evidence, reasoning, and uncertainty.

## Orchestration

Coordination of intent, context, planning, tools, workers, state, authority, verification, delivery, and recovery toward an outcome.

## Parallel work

Work divided so that components proceed concurrently or independently before synthesis. Merely issuing several calls is not enough if isolation, result structure, and synthesis are absent.

## Principal

A person or entity on whose behalf a system acts and whose authority, preferences, and confidential context bound the work.

## Protocol

A specified set of tasks, conditions, repetitions, and observations used to assess a claim.

## Read back

See destination read back.

## Regression

A later comparable observation showing that a previously supported mandatory behavior now fails or has materially weakened.

## Reliability

The repeatability of capability under relevant variation, including success rate, variance, detection of failure, and recovery.

## Resource claim

A recorded assertion of control or reserved access to a resource by an agent or process, ideally with owner, scope, start, heartbeat, expiry, and release state.

## Rubric

The versioned criteria and decision rules used to convert evidence into a verdict.

## Self report

A system's or operator's account of its own actions and outcomes. Self report can be useful evidence but is not independent proof.

## Side effect

A state change caused by an action, including changes that persist after the immediate task or affect another system or person.

## Snapshot

A bounded description of facts about a system at a date or over an interval, used as the explicit basis for an assessment.

## Steering

Direction supplied during ongoing work that changes, narrows, pauses, reprioritizes, or stops that work.

## Synthesis

A linked summary of the current best conclusion in a deliberation, including consensus, dissent, open questions, and verdict change history.

## Tell tale failure

A characteristic failure that reveals a tier property is absent even when surface behavior looked successful.

## Tier

One of nine levels in the orchestration ladder, from Tier 0 to Tier 8, defined by the kind of work and mandatory properties involved.

## Unknown outcome

An attempted action or observation whose result could not be established. Unknown is neither success nor failure.

## Verification

Observation adequate to establish that an intended state, output, or delivery actually occurred.

## Worker

An agent, process, device, or human assigned a bounded component of work by an orchestrator or coordinating procedure.

## Canary statement

A fabricated statement disclosed to exactly one witness through one channel under the sealed procedure in `docs/PROTOCOLS.md` (G4), used to detect whether a record path outside that channel carries statements. See CLM-016.

## Clean rate

The proportion of attempts confirmed with no duplicate effect, collateral effect, or intervention rescue, over all attempts including unknowns. See `docs/METRICS.md` section 2.

## Confirmation rate

Confirmed outcomes over confirmed, failed, and unknown outcomes combined. Unknowns remain in the denominator. See `docs/METRICS.md` section 2.

## Escalation quality

The measurable quality of an escalation independent of its rate: timing relative to irreversible steps, completeness of the decision context supplied, and routing to a principal entitled to decide. See CLM-015.

## Failure code

A short code (F-SRC to F-LEAK, defined in `docs/SCORECARDS.md`) attached to every failed gate so failures can be counted and compared across assessments.

## Near miss

A run with an acceptable final outcome whose path contained an event that would fail or harm under slightly different conditions, coded NM-1 to NM-4 in `docs/METRICS.md` section 5.

## Operation class

A category of fleet work distinguished by reversibility and consequence (for example, read only coordination versus irreversible actuation), used when verdicts are issued per class rather than per fleet. See CLM-020.
