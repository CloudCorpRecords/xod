# Tier 4: Research across sources, with synthesis

## Definition

Tier 4 covers investigation across multiple sources whose contents may be incomplete, stale, biased, or contradictory. The system must gather, weigh, reconcile, and synthesize while preserving the distinction among confirmed facts, inferences, and unknowns.

## What it looks like

- Several relevant sources are consulted, and their roles in the conclusion can be identified.
- Conflicts trigger deeper inspection rather than a vote or average.
- The final synthesis states what is established, what is inferred, what remains unresolved, and what evidence would change the conclusion.

## Prerequisites from lower tiers

- Tier 1 source selection and exact reporting.
- Enough domain method to distinguish primary records, secondary summaries, opinion, and promotional material.
- A procedure for recording failed or inaccessible sources as unread.

## Mandatory criteria

- Source selection covers the decisive perspectives and primary records reasonably available.
- Claims are attributed to sources with appropriate time and scope.
- Contradictions are investigated to underlying evidence or explicitly left unresolved.
- Confirmed facts, inferences, and unknowns are not blended.
- A failed source is not represented as an empty source.
- The synthesis answers the research question rather than dumping collected material.

## Observable evidence

- Source list with access times and evidence classes.
- Notes or artifacts showing how decisive conflicts were resolved.
- Citations tied to specific propositions rather than decorative links.
- Records of sources attempted but unread.
- Draft or structured findings that preserve uncertainty before final prose.

## Test protocol ideas

1. Pose questions with an independently established answer and at least one misleading secondary source.
2. Include sources that disagree because of date, definition, geography, or scope.
3. Make one relevant source unavailable and score whether the report narrows its confidence.
4. Require the system to identify what additional evidence would resolve the main uncertainty.
5. Repeat across domains to avoid rewarding familiarity with one topic.

## Borderline cases that do not qualify

- Collecting many links and summarizing them is research activity, not necessarily Tier 4 synthesis.
- Majority agreement among sources derived from the same origin is not independent corroboration.
- A correct conclusion with no treatment of a known contradiction is incomplete.
- A cautious report that lists only unknowns may be honest but does not satisfy synthesis if evidence supports more.
- Search snippets can guide inquiry but do not settle volatile destination facts.

## Tell tale failure modes

- The first plausible answer terminates the search.
- Averaging incompatible values or dates into a false compromise.
- Treating absence of search results as absence of the phenomenon.
- Losing source qualifiers during synthesis.
- Presenting an inference as a directly observed fact.

## Common false positives

- Citation density mistaken for verification.
- Several syndicated copies counted as independent sources.
- A polished narrative that hides unresolved contradictions.
- Human selection of sources so complete that the agent only summarizes.
- Benchmark questions whose answers appear in training data and require no live investigation.

## Open questions

- How should source independence be measured in a network of copied and derived material?
- When is it better to report a conflict than to resolve it?
- What synthesis format best preserves uncertainty without becoming unreadable?
- How should proprietary or inaccessible primary sources affect confidence?

## Measurement focus

The central measurement problem is evidential independence. Tier 4 is not about reading more pages. It is about knowing which observations actually add new support and which merely repeat an upstream claim.

## Assessment note

A tier assessment should name the configuration, environment, interval, protocol, and evidence packet. Observed behavior that resembles this tier should not be promoted to a reliable tier claim until every mandatory criterion has evidence or is explicitly marked as a limiting unknown. Failures belong in the assessment record; removing them would weaken, not strengthen, the claim.
